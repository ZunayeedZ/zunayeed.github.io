## About Me

I am a PhD researcher in Cybersecurity Engineering at George Mason University, with a research focus on machine learning, adversarial AI, and cybersecurity for aviation systems.

My research focuses on developing intelligent and scalable approaches for detecting and mitigating cyber threats in complex aviation environments. My current work explores distributed anomaly detection using multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) for GNSS security.

I am particularly interested in the intersection of:

- Cybersecurity
- Machine Learning and Artificial Intelligence
- Adversarial AI
- Reinforcement Learning
- GNSS and Aviation Security
- Distributed and Multi-Agent Systems

My research combines machine learning, simulation, statistical analysis, and cybersecurity to investigate the resilience of safety-critical systems against sophisticated cyber threats.

## Research

My current research investigates **GNSS distributed anomaly detection using multi-agent reinforcement learning in aviation environments**.

I am developing scalable experimental environments for studying GNSS jamming and spoofing and investigating how multiple aircraft can cooperate as distributed agents to detect and classify anomalies.

My broader research interests include:

- GNSS cybersecurity
- Aviation cybersecurity
- Anomaly and intrusion detection
- Adversarial machine learning
- Reinforcement learning
- Multi-agent reinforcement learning
- Cyber-physical systems
- AI for security

## Publications & Presentations

I have published and presented research in cybersecurity, machine learning, and aviation systems. My work includes research on scalable GNSS cybersecurity testbeds for analyzing the system-level effects of jamming and spoofing in aviation environments.
